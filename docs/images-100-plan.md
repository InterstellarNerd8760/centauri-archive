# The first 100 images: selection and acquisition rules

Status: **13 documentary images preserved, 91 still to acquire**. The 100-image plan is not a completed photo collection. Scientific diagrams are stored separately.

The canonical machine-readable list is `curation/images-100.json`. It defines 104 explicitly named image targets across ten categories. The public `images.html` reader displays them and distinguishes **included** records from **planned** subjects. Neither a placeholder nor a linked source candidate constitutes an archived image.

An image is selected to explain a concept or convey a specific observation, not merely to occupy a slot. The original goal of 100 is flexible. The collection includes optical photographs and labeled scientific images such as ultrasound. Original explanatory diagrams belong in the separate scientific library. Composite, simulated, false-color and schematic imagery must always be identified accurately.

## Minimum selection requirements

A selected photograph or illustration needs a verified original file, creator credit, date where known, exact source URL, rights statement, any required attribution, transformations and cropping disclosed, scientifically accurate caption, text alternative, and independent review. If identifiable people are depicted, check relevant privacy and publicity considerations; contributions of voices, children, or community-sensitive material require particular care and documented permission where necessary.

For NASA material, see https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/ . Most NASA science imagery is accessible for reuse under the published guidelines, but not all material or NASA branding is unrestricted. For Wikimedia Commons, verify the **individual file** conditions rather than assuming everything is public domain: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia .

The source-derived master image, web-optimized version, and potential flight-encoded image are separate. Do not simply embed hotlinked web images in the permanent archive. The flight subset may be smaller than 100 images if mass, encoding and redundancy tests demand it.

## Practical first selection sequence

1. Develop a basic bitstream/image primer in which the symbol mapping can be inferred; use original diagrams as scientific teaching material, not as photographic evidence.
2. Ingest source-verified NASA Earth and Solar System media plus original diagrams.
3. Add a representative set of ecosystems, microbes, organisms and landscapes.
4. Obtain appropriately licensed or consented cultural and human images with proper contextual review.
5. Test actual decoding from the chosen physical or digital carrier before declaring the set complete.

The category allocations are adjustable. Representativeness should be evaluated across all 100 images rather than equating equal geographic counts with equal cultural coverage.
