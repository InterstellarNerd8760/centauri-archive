# Real-world image and recording candidates

Status: source research on 2026-11-22. The images below are **not yet included** in the verifiable ZIP. This list does not authorize mass import. Check the exact source file, applicable reuse terms, credit, resolution, and any subjects' consent before ingesting.

## Image candidates (priority order)

1. **Apollo 17 full-disk Earth ("Blue Marble"), December 7, 1972**
   - Original credit: NASA / Apollo 17 crew / Project Apollo Archive.
   - Media and English caption source: https://astro4edu.org/en/resources/media/q124ZA8aq21/
   - The IAU/OAE page identifies the *image* as public domain. Its captions have separate CC BY 4.0 terms; use our own captions or attribute theirs.
   - Priority: high. Retain a preservation-quality master separately, include a clearly marked size-optimized derivative in an eventual compact edition.

2. **Apollo 11 Earth over the lunar horizon, July 20, 1969**
   - Source: https://science.nasa.gov/resource/apollo-11-mission-image-view-of-moon-limb-with-earth-on-the-horizon/
   - Credit: NASA / Apollo 11.
   - Usage: consult NASA's image and media guidelines. This is an excellent view of Earth from elsewhere, but a compact archive should not prioritize decorative historical photographs over explanatory imagery.

3. **NASA Blue Marble 2002 Earth observation composite**
   - Source: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-blue-marble-true-color-global-imagery-at-1km-resolution/
   - Credit: NASA Earth Observatory (see individual source credits).
   - Candidate for a complete global surface visual; it is a composite, not a single photograph and must be labeled as such.

4. **DNA double helix, "202202 DNA colored.svg"**
   - Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:202202_DNA_colored.svg
   - Author: DataBase Center for Life Science (DBCLS).
   - License shown: CC BY 4.0; source indicates its license has **not yet been reviewed by a Wikimedia reviewer**. Attribution is required. Hold until source verification, or prefer original diagrams.

5. **Water cycle (USGS)**

## Recording candidates

For the first sound experiment, create an **original synthesized waveform** with its exact sample encoding and measured frequency. This does not require a field-recording license. After that, collect original nature recordings (wind, rain, birds, ocean), human voices with recorded informed consent and attributed languages, and ordinary cultural sounds with suitable permissions. None are included yet. Avoid relying on streaming audio, copyrighted musical compositions or material without clear redistribution rights.

## Ingestion checklist

- Exact source URL, author, creation/retrieval date, revision and license
- Evidence of permission for redistribution and transformations
- Accurate description and no false provenance (photograph versus composite versus illustration)
- Master kept separately from a compact export; reproducible resizing and checksums
- Transparent metadata for any color correction, crop or lossy compression
- Reader controls for text alternatives, captions and transcripts
- Review status, cultural representation, consent and privacy, including for children
- Independent tests that the **flight encoding** is actually recoverable (a browser preview is not enough)
